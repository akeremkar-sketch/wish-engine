**Status:** done, 21 November 2026, on branch `physics-2d` (from `r1.1`).
Plan: [1.1-2d-and-online.md](1.1-2d-and-online.md) §4 and §6 (package 4);

## 1. Problem

The yardstick game of release 1.1 is a top-down 2D map of rooms, corridors
and doors with ten walking players; the 2D kit also has to serve side-view
platformers. Physics was 3D only: a game could lock the Z axis of each body
by hand (`RigidBody.lock_translation`/`lock_rotation`), but shapes, gravity,
the character controller and the queries all stayed 3D. A character walking
along a wall drifted along Z, a ball weighed as a sphere, things placed at
different depths (sprites on different layers) did not meet, and a ray had
to be aimed in 3D.

What a 2D profile must give:

- a setting, by addition, that makes a scene's physics 2D, with two kinds:
  **top-down** (no gravity, no ground) and **side view** (gravity along −Y);
- shapes that make sense in the plane: box, circle, capsule (a polygon if
  cheap);
- character movement that works in both kinds;
- `physics_query` (ray, overlap, touching) in the plane;
- determinism and snapshot/restore bit for bit, as in 3D;
- `check` warnings for setups that only make sense in 3D.

The engine's conventions hold: the 2D world is the X–Y plane, +Y up, the
camera looks along −Z (ADR-021).

## 2. Options

| Option | Plus | Minus |
|---|---|---|
| **A. The pinned 3D Rapier held to the plane** | No new dependency; one backend, one integration (sync, events, characters, platform carrying, queries, snapshots); determinism already proven on Windows and Linux in CI (ADR-014); every later physics feature comes to 2D for free | A 3D solver does 2D work: more costly per body; the plane has to be enforced (locks, placement, character moves) |
| **B. `rapier2d` as a second backend** | Native 2D: cheaper per body, exact 2D shapes (polygons, half-spaces), no drift out of the plane by construction | A second pinned dependency (ADR-028: upgraded only as its own package); a second integration of about the size of `wish_physics` (Rapier's 2D types differ: `Vec2`, scalar angles, other shapes), or generic code over both; a second determinism proof per platform; every physics fix and feature done twice |

## 3. Measurement

A top-down test map like the yardstick game's: a 48 × 32 m station of
**298 wall tiles** (1 m boxes: the outer wall, three walls across and two
along, with 2 m doors), **10 characters** walking with Rapier's kinematic
character controller (each turns every 1.5 s, from a fixed sequence, at
3.5 m/s), and dynamic crates and balls. Both backends at Rapier 0.36.1 with
`enhanced-determinism` (no SIMD, one thread), release build, the same scene
built the same way, best of three runs of 1,200 ticks after 60 ticks of
warm-up, on the development machine (Ryzen 5 5600, pinned to four cores).
The harness was a scratch crate outside the repository (it is the only
place `rapier2d` was compiled; it is not a dependency of the engine).

| Scene | 3D Rapier held to the plane | `rapier2d` |
|---|---|---|
| 358 bodies: 50 crates and balls at rest (damping 2) | step 0.017 ms + characters 0.085 ms = **0.102 ms per tick** | step 0.010 ms + characters 0.025 ms = **0.035 ms per tick** |
| 608 bodies: 300 crates and balls bouncing (no damping, restitution 0.8) | step 0.403 ms + characters 0.292 ms = **0.695 ms per tick** | step 0.180 ms + characters 0.088 ms = **0.268 ms per tick** |

(Characters as the profile moves them: "up" along Z, the Z part of each move
dropped. Runs on 21 November 2026 with other builds on the machine; earlier
runs of the same harness were within 10 % of these.)

- **Cost.** `rapier2d` is about 2.6 to 2.9 times cheaper. In absolute terms
  the 3D backend spends 0.1 ms of a 16.7 ms frame on the yardstick map
  (0.6 %) and 0.7 ms (4 %) with 300 bodies in motion, which is far more than a hidden-role
  party game moves. The character controller is the larger share in both.
- **Determinism.** Both give the same hash twice in a process, and both
  continue bit for bit from a cloned state (cloned at tick 200, continued to
  900). Only the 3D backend's results are already checked on Linux by CI.
- **Staying in the plane.** With the bodies locked (translation Z, rotation
  X and Y) the dynamic bodies never left the plane (largest |z| 0, no tilt).
  The **characters did**: Rapier's controller slides a round shape along
  contacts whose normals are not exactly in the plane, and the error grows.
  With the controller's "up" along Z, characters were up to 0.67 m off the
  plane after 1,200 ticks; with "up" along Y, 0.87 m, and one of the ten
  went around the end of a 1 m deep wall and out of the map. Dropping the Z
  part of every character move fixed it: after 1,200 ticks every character
  was in the map with |z| = 0 in both scenes. On the resting map 5 of the 10
  characters ended within 5 cm of where `rapier2d` put them; the others
  differ the way two solvers differ after many contacts (a different side
  of a door), not by escaping. That projection is part of the profile (§4).
- **Code cost.** Option A: about 350 lines with comments in `wish_physics` (one new module,
  `plane.rs`, and small changes in the step, the character move and the
  queries), one field and one enum in `wish_types`, two answers of
  `physics_query`, one `check` warning. Option B: a second backend of about
  the size of `wish_physics` (1,300 lines with the queries), a new dependency
  in the licence list and in `deny.toml`, and a second recorded hash per
  platform.

## 4. Decision

**Option A: the pinned 3D Rapier held to the X–Y plane.** The measurement
shows no clear problem: 2.6 times the cost of `rapier2d`, but well under 1 %
of a frame on the yardstick map; the drift out of the plane is real and is
fixed in the profile. One backend keeps one determinism proof and one place
for every fix. **Trigger** (when to measure `rapier2d` again): a 2D game
whose physics takes more than 4 ms per tick (25 % of the frame budget, the
3D budget of M6 §3.5), or a 2D feature that 3D shapes cannot give (concave
polygons from tiles are made of convex pieces, so tilemaps are not one).

How the profile works:

- **The setting.** `PhysicsSettings.profile: PhysicsProfile` = `Full3D`
  (the default, every existing file), `TopDown2D` or `SideView2D`. A field
  added with a default: scene files are byte-identical. The scaffold of 2D
  projects (package 1) sets it.
- **The plane.** In a 2D profile every dynamic and kinematic body is locked
  to the plane (translation Z, rotation X and Y), whatever its own
  `RigidBody` locks say. Every body is placed in Rapier at **z = 0**, turned
  only by the part of its rotation about Z (its twist). When physics writes
  a body back, the entity's own z and any tilt about X or Y are kept, so a
  sprite drawn on another layer (another z) still collides with the walls,
  and a tilted sprite keeps its tilt.
- **Shapes** are their outline in the plane, 1 m deep: `Box` a rectangle of
  `size.x` × `size.y`, `Sphere` a circle (diameter the larger of `size.x` and
  `size.y`), `Capsule` a rounded rectangle along Y (turn the entity about Z
  to lay it down), `Cylinder` the rectangle seen from its side (`check`
  warns). `size.z` and the z of `offset` are not used. A shape weighs its
  **density × area × 1 m** with the moment of inertia of the flat outline
  about Z (Rapier's 3D mass would make a disc weigh as a ball); a 1 m box
  weighs the same in 2D and 3D.
- **Gravity.** Top-down: none (`gravity` is not used). Side view: `gravity`
  without its z part.
- **Characters.** Side view: the 3D controller unchanged (ground along +Y,
  slopes, steps, snapping, moving platforms), in the plane. Top-down: no
  ground: one move in the plane with Rapier's "up" along Z (so every wall is
  too steep to climb), no steps, no snapping, `grounded` always false,
  `blocked` on any contact. In both, what a move does along Z is dropped
  (§3: without it characters leave the plane around wall ends) and the
  wanted velocity's z is not used.
- **Queries** (`PhysicsState::raycast`/`overlap`, used by `physics_query`
  and by game code's `raycast`) are asked in the plane: the z of positions
  and directions is not used, a ray along Z is an error with a hint, hits
  are at z = 0. `physics_query` takes `[x, y]` for positions, directions and
  box half sizes in a 2D profile, `{circle: r}` as a synonym of `sphere`, and
  names the profile in its answer.
- **Changing the profile** while a session runs builds the physics state
  again (contacts are found and reported again).
- **`check`** warns `physics_3d_in_2d` at the field: a `Cylinder` collider,
  gravity written in a top-down scene, a gravity with a z part in a side-view
  scene.

Not done here:

- **Polygons.** A collider is a fixed-size component (`Plain`, invariant 1);
  a polygon needs a list of points of any length. It needs either a shape
  file (like material files) or a fixed array that every collider would
  carry. Tilemap collision (package 5) builds walls from boxes, so the
  yardstick game does not need it; slopes for platformers come with the
  tilemap package, which owns per-tile shapes.
- A 2D picture of colliders (`capture_frame view: colliders` draws the 3D
  shapes, which seen along −Z are the 2D outlines already).
- Mixing 2D and 3D physics in one scene: the profile is per scene (per
  world).

## 5. Test plan and evidence

- `wish_physics/tests/plane.rs`: walls stop a top-down character (walls at
  another depth than the character, its own z kept, no gravity, sliding
  along a wall into a corner, a move along Z does nothing); a capsule turned
  flat does not fit through a door it fits through upright; a side-view
  character walks, jumps about 1.8 m (6 m/s under 9.81 m/s²) and lands, its
  z kept; a body thrown along Z and spun about X and Y stays in the plane and
  rests on a floor at another depth; a tilted sprite keeps its tilt; nothing
  falls seen from above; a disc weighs its area; rays and overlaps in the
  plane, before and after a step; changing the profile rebuilds physics;
  the station map (298 walls, 10 walking characters, 50 bouncing bodies)
  continues bit for bit after a snapshot and gives the same hash in two
  apps; **a recorded hash of the station after 600 ticks** (CI checks it on
  Windows and Linux, debug and release).
- `wish_physics/tests/plane.rs::what_a_2d_tick_costs` (`#[ignore]`, release):
  the station through the engine (§6).
- `wish_control/tests/physics_2d.rs`: `physics_query` in a 2D scene and
  session (`[x, y]`, `circle`, the profile in the answer, errors); `check`
  warns `physics_3d_in_2d` at the right fields and not in 3D.
- The 3D path is unchanged: the recorded pyramid hash holds, every 3D test of
  `wish_physics` passes, and the content of a 3D scene is bit for bit the
  same as before (§6; the pile's world hash moved only with the registry).

## 6. Results

- **Through the engine** (`what_a_2d_tick_costs`, release, the station map
  with the ECS sync, write-back and events): 358 bodies with 50 at rest
  **0.36 ms per tick** (2 % of a 60 Hz frame); 608 bodies with 300 bouncing
  **1.03 ms** (6 %). The difference to §3 is the engine's own work per entity
  (sync and write-back), the same in 3D.
- **Recorded hashes.** The station after 600 ticks:
  `b3fa11a53f26b7c011c908f573737727`, the same in debug and release on
  Windows (CI checks Linux). The 3D **pile** world hash of
  `wish_physics/tests/world.rs` moved from `ffb4e843…` to `b31d9ba9…`: the
  world hash includes the registry's fingerprint, and `PhysicsSettings` has a
  new field. The physics itself did not change: a scratch test hashed only
  the bits of every `Transform`, `BodyVelocity` and `CharacterState` of a 3D
  scene (falling boxes and balls, a step, a character walking with a Z
  component) after 600 ticks, built from `r1.1` and from this branch in
  separate build folders: `95a69f92b827386a` both, with the same 52 contact
  events. The raw Rapier pyramid hash (`proof.rs`) did not move.
- **Defaults.** `crates/wish_scene/src/defaults/format-2.json` gained
  `PhysicsSettings.profile: "Full3D"` (`UPDATE_DEFAULTS=1`); `format-1.json`
  is unchanged. The look-lock tests now read a file of format 1 with the
  record of format 1 plus every field added since at its default, since an
  addition has no other meaning in an older file (this was the first
  addition after the switch).
- **Agents.** The guide's Physics section, the `PhysicsSettings` search
  words (2d, top-down, platformer...), a concept hint for names from other
  engines (`rigidbody2d`, `circlecollider2d`, `characterbody2d`...), the "A 2D
  game" recipe (now `SideView2D`, no per-body locks) and a new recipe, **"A
  top-down character"**, which the recipes test builds, applies and plays
  (the wall stops the player, it slides into the corner, it keeps its depth).
