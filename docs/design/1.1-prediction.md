# Release 1.1, package 11: prediction, reconciliation, interpolation, tick sync

Plan: [1.1-2d-and-online.md](1.1-2d-and-online.md) §3 and §6, package 11.
Architecture: ADR-029 in [../DECISIONS.md](../DECISIONS.md). Builds on
replication ([1.1-replication.md](1.1-replication.md)), the transport
([1.1-net-transport.md](1.1-net-transport.md)) and players
([1.1-players.md](1.1-players.md)).

Recommended model and effort: Opus 5.5, xhigh (a foundation package of the
networking area).

Status: built on 22 November 2026 on the branch `prediction` (from `r1.1`).
The code is in `wish_replicate` (`sync`, `predict`, `interpolate`); its tests
are listed in §9 and pass (`cargo test -p wish_replicate`).

## 1. Problem

Over 100 ms of latency, a client that only mirrors the host shows its own
player 200 ms after the key was pressed: the input travels to the host, the
host simulates it, the state travels back. That is not playable. ADR-029
answers with three parts, and this package builds them:

- **Tick sync.** The client runs its own simulation *ahead* of the host, by
  the round trip plus a margin, so an input stamped with the client's tick
  reaches the host before the host simulates that tick. The distance moves
  smoothly with the network, and the client's time never goes backwards.
- **Prediction and reconciliation.** The client runs the game's own systems
  on its own copy of the world from the last state the host confirmed plus
  its own inputs not confirmed yet, and shows its own player from that copy
  at once. When a confirmed state arrives it compares; on a difference it
  goes back to the confirmed state and plays its unconfirmed inputs again.
  With nothing lost the prediction equals the host's result bit for bit
  (determinism, ADR-029), so nothing is ever corrected. A correction that
  does happen is smoothed on screen, never in the simulated state.
- **Interpolation.** Everyone else's entities are shown a fixed delay behind
  the host, between two received states, so they move smoothly despite
  jitter and loss; past the newest state they are extrapolated a little and
  then held.

All of it must stay deterministic (time from ticks and the caller's
`now_ms`, ordered collections only), bounded against a hostile or broken
peer, and observable.

## 2. Options

**Where the prediction runs.**

1. *In the client's mirror world.* One world, but replication writes the
   host's states into it while the prediction writes its own: every message
   would undo the prediction, and the systems would also move entities the
   client cannot predict (other players). Rejected.
2. *A world of its own, the prediction world, inside an `App` the caller
   injects.* **Chosen.** The caller builds an `App` with the game's types
   and systems, exactly as the host builds its own (the same function, in
   the tests). The mirror world stays the exact copy of what the host sent
   (`Client::mirror` still equals `Host::target`); the prediction world holds
   a copy of every mirrored entity and is simulated ahead. `App` snapshots
   carry the world, the tick and every system's random stream, so going
   back is `App::restore`.

**When to simulate again.**

1. *Every frame* (rollback style): restore the newest confirmed state and
   replay up to the present each frame. Simple, always fresh, but the client
   pays the whole prediction window (about 20 ticks at 100 ms) every frame.
2. *Only on a difference* (Overwatch, Rocket League): keep the predicted
   history per tick; when the host confirms tick `t`, compare the predicted
   entities at `t`; replay only when they differ. **Chosen:** with nothing
   lost it never replays, which is also the strongest test of exactness
   (zero corrections means every predicted tick equalled the host's).

**What is predicted.**

1. *Only what the local player owns.* A box the player pushes would be
   shown late and the avatar would sink into it.
2. *Owned entities and what they are near.* **Chosen:** the entities the
   local player owns (`NetOwner`), plus unowned entities with a `Transform`
   within `touch_radius` (default 2 m) of an owned one; one leaves the set
   beyond 1.5 times that (hysteresis). Entities owned by *another* player are
   never predicted (their inputs are unknown here): they are interpolated.
   The radius must cover the contact distance plus how far the player moves
   in the prediction window, so a box joins the set before the prediction
   touches it (§3.4).
3. *A game-written marker.* More control, but a new engine type and one more
   thing to get wrong; the rule of 2 needs none. Left for when a game needs
   it.

**Tick sync.**

1. *A fixed lead* (replication's `input_lead`): wrong for every latency but
   one.
2. *Round trip plus a margin, from measurements.* The client estimates the
   host's clock from the ticks of arriving state messages (the fastest ones,
   as NTP does), adds the measured round trip (`Endpoint::rtt_ms`), the
   measured jitter and a margin, and steers its own simulation rate towards
   that target. Right while the network is steady; but the transport's
   round trip is a slow average (a ping every 500 ms, one eighth per
   sample), so after a rise in latency inputs come late for seconds.
3. *Host feedback.* Every state message already says which of the player's
   frames the host applied (`input ack`): when the host confirms tick `t`
   with an older frame although the frame of `t` was sent, that frame came
   late, and by how much. **Chosen: 2, corrected by 3.** No new field is
   needed; a late report raises the lead at once by the lateness plus a
   tick, and the extra wears off slowly. The host's own count of late frames
   (§3.8) is the check that it works.

**Crate.** A crate beside `wish_replicate` would need the client's received
states by tick, its frames and its acknowledgements: either they become
public API of replication, or the two crates are one in all but name.
**Chosen: modules of `wish_replicate`** (`sync`, `predict`, `interpolate`),
which gains one dependency, `wish_app` (it points down: `wish_app` knows
only the core, the ECS, the schema and the plain types).

## 3. Decision

### 3.1 Ticks

The host simulates tick `t` with its `App` at tick `t` (`App::tick()` is `t`
before the step), applies its players' inputs for `t` first
(`Host::apply_inputs(world, t)`), and sends the state of `t` after the step.
The prediction app is aligned to the same numbers: before predicting tick
`s` its `App::tick()` is `s`, so the systems see the same `Time` as on the
host. `App::set_tick` is new for this (it keeps the world and the systems'
state).

### 3.2 Tick sync (`sync::TickSync`)

All arithmetic is in integer microseconds and thousandths of a tick.

- **Host clock.** For each new newest host tick `h` seen at local time `a`
  (the frame's `now_ms`), the sample `h · tick − a`. The largest of the last
  32 samples (half a second at 60 Hz) is the offset (it comes from the
  fastest messages); the mean distance of the samples below it is the
  jitter.
- **Target.** `T = ceil((now + offset + rtt + jitter + late + margin) /
  tick)`: `now + offset` is the host's tick as the fastest message would
  show it; one round trip covers the way back to the host and the delay of
  that message; jitter and `margin_ms` (default two ticks, 34 ms at 60 Hz)
  cover slower datagrams and one lost input datagram (every input message
  also carries the seven earlier frames not confirmed); `late` is the
  feedback below.
- **The round trip.** The client does not predict before the transport has
  measured one (`RTT_WAIT_US`, at most one second after the host's clock is
  known; then `assumed_rtt_ms`, 100, stands in). Until then it shows the
  newest confirmed state.
- **Late reports.** When the host confirms tick `t` with an applied frame
  `a < t`, and the frame of `t` was sent, the lead grows at once by
  `(t − a + 1)` ticks (`TickSync::late`). Reports about frames sent before
  that rise took effect are counted but add nothing (they describe the old
  lead). The extra is at most one second and wears off by a tick every 64
  frames.
- **Steering.** The predicted tick `P` plus the fraction owed is compared
  with `T`. The simulation advances by the frame's elapsed time times a
  rate: behind, up to 25 % faster (`error / 8` thousandths: late inputs
  cost more than a slightly fast clock); ahead, at most 10 % slower (50 %
  when more than `jump_ticks` ahead), never stopped. Time owed is turned
  into whole ticks, at most `max_steps` (4) per frame, and never more than
  one tick is banked.
- **Jumps only forward.** When `P` is more than `jump_ticks` (4) behind
  `T` (the start, a stall, a sudden rise in latency) it jumps to `T`, at
  most `max_catch_up` (120) ticks in one frame. Time is never moved
  backwards: a client too far ahead only slows down.
- **Ticks that cannot arrive in time.** The plan also says from which tick
  on a frame sent now reaches the host in time (`T` less the margin). A
  tick before that (the start, a jump, a client fallen behind) is predicted
  with the actions in force before, and its frame is not sent: the host
  holds the frame in force for every tick it has no newer frame for, which
  are exactly those actions, so even a jump predicts exactly. The frame's
  own actions start with the first tick that arrives in time
  (`PredictStats::held_ticks`).
- **Observable:** `SyncState` (target, lead over the newest host tick seen,
  rate, error, offset, jitter, round trip used, late extra, late reports),
  `PredictStats::jumps` and `held_ticks`; on the host,
  `HostStats::late_frames` and `Host::input_slack(player)` (§3.8).

### 3.3 Inputs

Each predicted tick `s` gets the frame's actions; they go into the
prediction world's `PlayerActionState` for the local player through the same
function the host uses (`input::write_players`: the same cleaning and
edges), are kept by tick until the host confirms them, and are sent stamped
`s` (`Client::queue_frame` + `Client::send_queued`, with the acknowledgements
as before). The host applies the newest frame stamped at or before its tick,
so an input that arrives in time is applied at exactly the tick the client
predicted it at.

### 3.4 The prediction world and what is predicted

The prediction world (the injected `App`'s world) holds one entity per
network id the client mirrors, with the replicated components only. Each
mirrored entity is one of:

- **predicted**: owned by the local player, or unowned and near (§2). It is
  simulated ahead, compared at confirmation, and shown from the prediction;
- **context**: everything else. It is in the prediction world so that the
  systems see it (a wall, another avatar), set to its newest confirmed state
  every time one arrives, never compared, and never shown from there.

Membership is decided after every predicted tick from the prediction
world's positions, and recorded per tick with the predicted states, so a
confirmation of tick `t` compares exactly what was predicted at `t`.

### 3.5 Reconciliation

A state message tells the client whether it is **complete** (new: nothing
the player may see was left out by the budget; §3.8). When the client reads
a complete message whole, and it is newer than the last one, the client
records its **confirmed view**: per entity, its newest state at or before
that tick (exact, since nothing was deferred). The predictor takes it each
frame:

- **First confirmation:** the prediction world gets every entity of it, the
  app's tick becomes `t + 1`, and the sync's first jump carries `P` ahead
  (with held actions, §3.2). Until the round trip is known each new
  confirmation simply replaces it.
- **Compare:** the predicted entities' states at `t` (kept per tick) against
  the confirmed view. Equal (the case with nothing lost): nothing is
  replayed; context entities take their confirmed states; history and
  inputs up to `t` are dropped.
- **Correct** (different, or the membership at `t` is not what the host
  shows): restore the app snapshot taken after predicting `t`, write the
  confirmed state of every entity (spawning, despawning, removing
  components), and predict `t + 1 ..= P` again with the kept inputs. Counted
  in `PredictStats::corrections`, with the largest positional difference.
- **Behind:** if `t` is beyond `P` (a stall), the prediction world is set to
  the confirmed state and `P` becomes `t` (counted as `rebases` when the
  prediction had been ahead).
- **Too far ahead:** the prediction never runs more than `max_ahead` (120)
  ticks past the newest confirmation; it waits (`stalls`) rather than
  extrapolating blindly.

What a replay cannot make exact, and is corrected like any difference:
systems that depend on the order entities are visited in (the prediction
world's entity ids are its own), systems that draw from their random
stream (the host's streams are not sent), systems that read components that
are not replicated (the prediction world has only the replicated ones),
resources (not replicated: the prediction world's resources are its own,
restored with the snapshots), and entities the prediction's systems spawn
themselves (they are not mirrored and never shown). `NetOwner` must be
replicated for anything to be predicted; without it every entity is
interpolated.

### 3.6 Smoothing

A correction, or an entity switching between predicted and interpolated,
would make it jump on screen. The presentation keeps a per-entity offset:
at the discontinuity, offset = where it was drawn − where it is now; every
frame the offset shrinks by `smoothing` (0.2) times the frame's length in
ticks, and below 0.1 mm it is zero. Beyond `snap_distance` (4 m) there is no
smoothing (a teleport stays a teleport). The offset lives in the
presentation only; the simulated state is never touched. With 0.2 per tick
an offset of 1 m is under 0.1 mm after 42 ticks (0.8^42 ≈ 8.5 · 10^-5).

### 3.7 Interpolation and extrapolation (`interpolate`)

Entities that are not predicted are shown at the display time
`D = host clock − interpolation_delay` (default 100 ms, six ticks), from the
states the client keeps per entity by tick (replication keeps up to 64). A
complete message read whole also says that every entity it did not carry is
unchanged up to its tick, so a still entity is known to be still. Between
the two known points around `D` the `Transform` is interpolated
(translation and scale linearly, rotation by the shorter way, normalized);
other components show the older state. Past the newest known point it is
extrapolated from the last two known points for at most
`max_extrapolation_ms` (100 ms) and then held; an entity known to be still
is held at once. The result, per network id, is a `Shown` (the transform to
draw, its source `Predicted | Interpolated | Extrapolated | Held`, and the
smoothing offset in it), read with `Predictor::presented()`. The renderer
integration (`wen play`, the editor's Play with N players) is package 14.

### 3.8 Changes to replication

- **Wire format version 2.** The state header gains one byte after the
  input acknowledgement: `u8 flags`, bit 0 = complete (no entity deferred,
  no removal cut by the budget); other bits must be 0. The welcome's
  version byte is 2; a client of version 1 refuses a version-2 welcome.
- **Client:** `queue_frame(tick, actions)` and `send_queued(endpoint)` for
  stamped frames (the old `send_input` is kept for clients that do not
  predict); `newest_seen()`; the confirmed view; the complete ticks kept for
  interpolation.
- **Host:** `HostStats::late_frames` (frames that arrived after the host had
  simulated their tick) and `Host::input_slack(player)` (the newest frame of
  that player the host holds, minus its current tick: how early inputs
  arrive).

## 4. Budgets

- **Memory per client:** one app snapshot per predicted tick not confirmed
  yet: about `(rtt + margin) / tick` snapshots (20 at 100 ms latency, at
  most `max_ahead` = 120) of the prediction world, which is the size of what
  the player may see.
- **Time per frame:** one or two ticks of the game's systems; a correction
  replays the window (about 20 ticks at 100 ms). The tests count every
  replayed tick (`PredictStats::replayed_ticks`, `longest_replay`).
- **Bandwidth:** unchanged, one byte per state message more.
- **Host:** nothing new per tick; a slow client never makes the host wait
  (it holds that player's last input; §9).

## 5. Limits, stated

- Physics: the prediction runs whatever schedule the caller gives; the
  physics engine's internal state is part of the app snapshot (a world
  extension), but whether predicted Rapier bodies match the host's is
  measured with the yardstick game (package 15), not here.
- Only `Transform` is interpolated; the smoothing offset is a translation.
- Spawns made by predicted systems are not matched with the host's later
  entities.
- Other players' inputs are never predicted (held at rest in the
  prediction world); interaction with another player's avatar is corrected
  when the host's state arrives.
- The net tool, sessions with players and the editor's network panel are
  package 14; they will put `PredictStats`, `SyncState`, `HostStats` and
  the presentation into the control plane.

## 6. Diagnostics

No new codes. A prediction world that lacks a replicated type, or has it
with another layout, is `net_types_mismatch`; a setting out of range is
`net_config_invalid`; a system that fails while predicting is the app's own
`system_failed` (returned in the frame's report).

## 7. Recorded values

No engine type is added, so no world hash and no defaults file moves. The
replication wire version moves from 1 to 2 (unreleased).

## 8. Not done here

See §5. Also: the per-frame actions are the same for every tick a frame
predicts (a frame that runs two ticks repeats its input), which matches how
a game samples input once per frame.

## 9. Tests

`cargo test -p wish_replicate --test prediction` (and the unit tests of
`sync` and `interpolate`). A small deterministic game: avatars walk by
their player's `move` action at 3 m/s; a box is pushed out of an avatar's
way. The host and every client build their app with the same function. One
host and up to four clients over a `SimNetwork` in one process.

- **No loss, 100 ms latency each way:** zero corrections on every client;
  every tick each client predicted equals the host's state of that tick for
  every predicted entity, compared value by value (bytes, texts and
  references); player 1 pushes the box, and the box is predicted while it is
  pushed; no input frame reaches the host late.
- **100 ms latency, 30 ms jitter, 5 % loss, duplication and reordering**
  (`LinkConditions::BAD`): corrections are rare (at most 2 % of the
  confirmations; measured: none, §10), every correction is followed by an
  exact confirmation within 8 frames, the smoothing offset is back to zero
  within 42 frames of the last correction or change of what is predicted,
  once the bots stop every client's prediction equals the host, and at most
  1 % of the input frames after the start come late.
- **Lost inputs:** twice, everything one player sends is lost for 20 frames
  while it turns: the prediction is corrected, the last correction comes at
  most 40 frames after a burst ends, none between the bursts, the smoothing
  offset is gone within 42 frames, and every later tick equals the host's.
- **Determinism:** two runs with the same seed give the same mirror-world
  and prediction-app hashes at every tick and the same statistics; another
  seed gives other hashes.
- **Tick sync:** the predicted tick never goes backwards; the lead settles
  near the round trip plus the margin; when latency rises from 50 ms to
  150 ms mid-match the client catches up (a jump or a faster rate) and late
  frames stay rare.
- **A lagging or hostile client cannot make the host wait:** one client's
  link has 750 ms of latency each way and 30 % loss, another sends input
  for another player, input stamped far ahead and garbage; the host
  simulates one tick per frame throughout, the honest client predicts with
  zero corrections, and the lagging one is corrected and is exact once the
  bots stop.
- **Interpolation:** a remote avatar walking at constant speed is drawn
  between the states around the display time, about the delay behind, and
  moves every frame by a nearly constant step despite jitter; when its
  updates stop it is extrapolated for at most 100 ms and then held.

## 10. Measurements

`cargo test -p wish_replicate --test prediction -- --nocapture` on 22
November 2026 (debug build, Windows; the simulator is deterministic, so the
numbers are the same on every run). Ticks of 16 ms.

| Scenario | Result |
|---|---|
| No loss, 100 ms each way, 3 players, 480 frames | 0 corrections; 437 confirmations, all exact; 429 predicted ticks per player compared with the host value by value, 0 different; 0 late input frames; lead 15 ticks (round trip 14 + margin) |
| `BAD` (100 ms, 30 ms jitter, 5 % loss, 2 % duplicates, 10 % reordering), 3 players, 900 frames | 0 corrections in 522 to 560 confirmations per player; 0 predicted ticks different from the host; 1 late frame of 2 433 input messages; lead 19 to 20 ticks |
| Two bursts of 20 frames in which every input of player 1 is lost, while it turns every 30 ticks (`BAD` otherwise) | 6 corrections, all within 13 frames of the end of the second burst (the first burst fell between turns); longest replay 20 ticks; largest correction 200 mm; smoothing offset gone 37 frames after the last correction |
| Latency 50 → 150 ms each way at frame 300 | lead 9 → 21 ticks; 118 late frames (both players) while the transport's round trip catches up, none in the last 300 frames; 0 corrections |
| One client at 750 ms each way and 30 % loss, one hostile client, one honest client on a perfect link, 1 100 frames | the host simulated one tick every frame; the honest client: 0 corrections, 1 091 ticks compared, 0 different; the lagging client: 30 corrections of 280 confirmations while its round trip was learnt (longest replay 105 ticks), exact after the bots stopped; the hostile one's forged input was dropped (`net_input_wrong_player`, `net_input_out_of_window`) and moved nobody |
| Interpolation of a remote walker (`BAD`) | 350 of 350 frames interpolated; error against the host's path at the display time: median 0, largest 4 · 10^-6 m; display time 13.25 ticks behind the host (about one way, 6.25 ticks, plus the 100 ms delay, 6.25 ticks) |
| Its updates cut | 13 frames still interpolated (the delay), 6 extrapolated, then held; the prediction waited at `max_ahead` (`stalls`) |

Under the yardstick conditions the prediction was never corrected: late
inputs are what corrects a prediction of one's own player, and tick sync
with the late reports and the held ticks kept them out. Corrections come
from inputs that never arrive (the burst test), from a client that has not
learnt its round trip yet (the lagging one), and, in a real game, from
other players touching what this player predicts.
